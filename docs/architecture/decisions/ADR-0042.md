# ADR-0042: Conversational directives resolve into the same governed operations as the controls; narrowing applies, widening and standing changes are confirmed

**Status:** PROPOSED (the user's directive of 2026-09-30, "additional architectural requirements", §A, §B, §J; awaiting approval) · **Date:** 2026-09-30 · Implementation: P19.5 Core slice (Pause all and priority verbs), P19 (grammar and write path for accounts and harnesses, chat approve/reject as P16 seam S3, the `route_explanation` card), P21 (answers over live work), P22 (model and location directive kinds) · Extends ADR-0006 and ADR-0015 · Reconciliation: [ARCHEUS_V1_CONVERSATIONAL_ORCHESTRATION.md](../ARCHEUS_V1_CONVERSATIONAL_ORCHESTRATION.md) A1, B1, C2, D4

## Decision

1. **Grammar first** (ADR-0006). The deterministic grammar gains verbs whose objects resolve
   exactly: `pause all`, `resume all`, `emergency stop` (with confirmation), `stop <running
   mission>` (one confirmation card doing pause then cancel — no new lifecycle edge),
   `approve` / `reject` bound to the `action_hash` of the card on screen (P16 S3),
   `reprioritise <mission>`, `use <account|harness>` / `don't use <account|harness>` for a named or
   the single active mission, `why <object>`, and `status` / `what is running`. No model is
   called; an object that does not resolve to exactly one thing falls through to the brain.
2. **The brain proposes a `resource_directive`** inside `intent.v1`: its scope (this request, a
   mission, a project, standing), prefer and forbid lists (accounts, harnesses, models,
   locations), a tier requirement, a pin. Core resolves every name to a real id; an unresolved or
   ambiguous name is a clarification card, never a guess. A user's "strongest / best model" is
   the top-tier requirement, or a pin when a model is named; "cheapest acceptable" is the router's
   default (the smallest tier meeting the requirement).
3. **Asymmetric application.**
   - A **narrowing** directive in the user's own request — forbid an account, harness, model or
     location; a tighter cost band — applies to the mission that request creates or continues,
     shown on its card as coming from the message and reversible there.
   - A **widening** directive — naming a `last_resort` or `explicit_only` account (ADR-0041), a
     pin, a higher tier or cost band — and **every standing change** (project defaults, a
     ResourcePolicy, a routing rule) is a PROPOSED change card that a user device confirms; the
     policy route keeps its `admin` scope. The brain's principal writes nothing.
   - Each applied directive records its `message_id` and `intent_id` on the preference or policy
     version it produced.
4. **State is answered from Core.** "What is running", "what is consuming my accounts" and "why"
   are answered from the live-work and consumption reads (ADR-0043) and the RouteDecision, with the
   `status` and `route_explanation` cards; the brain may paraphrase, the card is the record.
5. **Verbs act only for user principals**; text from fetched content, meeting notes or agent output
   is never parsed as a command; destructive verbs keep their confirmation and step-up.

## Why

The conversation is the primary interface (the product definition; ADR-0015), yet no
conversational path reaches what the router reads: `intent.v1` has no resource field
(`archeus/core/brain/intent.py:32-59`), a `preference` becomes a knowledge candidate
(`archeus/core/application/conversation.py:355-385`), and `approve`/`reject`/`reprioritise` are
recognised and declined (`archeus/core/application/grammar.py:26-28`). The asymmetry is what makes
a model-read directive safe: narrowing can only remove candidates — it may block work, never widen
authority — while anything that widens use or persists beyond the request passes through the same
confirmation and scope a button would.

## Alternatives

Confirm every directive (rejected as default: a user who says "don't use the shared account"
should not be asked to confirm a restriction; kept as the alternative in decision D-16); let the
brain write mission preferences directly (rejected: its principal may only propose); a separate
"assistant" command channel (rejected: a parallel system that bypasses the grammar and cards).

## Tradeoffs

A misread narrowing directive can block a mission until the user removes it from the card.

## Consequences

Amends plan §11 (intent pipeline step 2: the directive), domain-model §6 (Intent gains
`resource_directive`; card types emitted include `route_explanation`), the grammar table
(P7 tests), and ui-architecture §4.1 and §4.7. P24 gains the cross-phase question: "can any
message, brain reading or directive widen resource use or approve anything without a user-device
confirmation?" — no, by construction. G1–G5 of the reconciliation are its gates.
